How Vector Embeddings Work

From raw text to numbers — the engine behind RAG, semantic search, and AI retrieval

Step 01 — The Core Idea

Words become numbers

An embedding model reads a word or sentence and outputs a list of numbers called a vector. These numbers capture meaning — not spelling. Similar meanings → similar numbers.

Step 02 — Inside the Model

What is an embedding model?

It's a neural network — layers of mathematical functions. Text goes in one end, and a fixed-length list of numbers comes out the other. The model was trained on billions of sentences so it "knows" what words mean.

Step 03 — Vector Space

Similar words cluster together

Once everything is a vector, the model can measure distance between them. Words with similar meaning land close together in this space. This is what makes search work — your query lands near relevant documents.

Step 05 — How They're Built

How do people train embedding models?

The model learns from massive datasets of text. It's trained using a technique where it tries to predict: "these two sentences are similar" vs "these two are unrelated." Over millions of examples, it learns to map meaning into number-space.

🏋️

Contrastive Learning

Train on pairs: "a dog barked" and "the puppy made noise" → SIMILAR. "a dog barked" and "stock prices rose" → DIFFERENT. Model adjusts numbers until similar pairs are close.

🧠

Pre-trained on Billions of Docs

Models like BERT read Wikipedia, books, websites. They learn context: words near each other tend to be related — this is called "self-supervised" learning.

🎯

Fine-tuned for Retrieval

General models are then fine-tuned on question-answer pairs to make them great specifically at matching questions to relevant documents.
